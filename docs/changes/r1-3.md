## 2. W-002: H5 for a device holds through a disconnect and counts only connected time

**Root cause.** `health.go` made the H5 device finding only while `connected && remoteState == "valid" && percent < 100`. At a disconnect the server stays up, so the freezer did not hold it: the engine deleted the observation and resolved the record (a false "resolved" at every reboot), and the timer started again at the next connection. With `stuck_sync` 24 hours a PC that is off every night never got H5. Syncthing reports `remoteState: unknown` for a disconnected device, and no `FolderCompletion` events for it.

**What changed.**
- `internal/model/model.go`: `Server.LastCompletions` (folder → device → `Completion`) and `Server.LastCompletion(folder, dev)`: the completion that the server reported last while the device was connected and its state was valid. `RememberRemoteStates` (called by the hub after every update) records it next to `RemoteStates` and forgets both for unshared folders and devices (`forgetUnshared`). RAM only, like r0-i. Deep copy in `Clone`.
- `internal/model/finding.go`: `Finding.Held` (`json:"-"`, not stored): the finding is made from a last known state.
- `internal/rules/health.go` (`folderHealth`, new `stuckDevice`): connected (and its server up) with a valid state: judged from live data as before. Away (not connected, its server not up, or the state not reported yet) with a last known state `valid`: H5 is made from the last connected view and marked `Held`. After a restart of syncwatch there is no such view; then the completion that Syncthing reports now for the device is used (also `Held`).
- `internal/engine/engine.go`: a held finding is treated like a frozen one: an open record stays open and is marked stale, it never resolves, and no new record opens. The timer of its observation stops (`hold`/`release`, map `holds`): when the hold ends, the observation's `Since` moves forward by the time held. While a hold lasts, the moved `Since` is written at most every 10 minutes (`heldPersist`), so a restart of syncwatch counts at most 10 minutes of held time as stuck. A held finding starts no observation.

**DECISION (re-opens the r0-i item 2 DECISION "H5 keeps the live value"):** H5 for a device keeps the view of the last connection while the device is away, and counts only connected time towards `stuck_sync`. The observation's start is not kept through a disconnect (that would give a PC switched off mid-sync on Friday a false H5 on Monday).
**DECISION:** the connections of a server that is not up are old data: H5 for a device seen from an unreachable server is held too (its timer stops while the server is down, also after the server is back until the device reconnects).
**DECISION:** after a restart of syncwatch (no last connected view in RAM), the completion that Syncthing reports now for an away device keeps an open H5 held, instead of resolving it. The time that syncwatch itself was not running counts as stuck time, as for every other observation.
**DECISION:** the record's displayed "since" is the observation's start after the moves, so it can be later than the first time the device was seen stuck.

**User-visible.** An open H5 for a PC stays open with the tag `stale` while the PC is off, a NAS restarts or syncwatch restarts; no "resolved" history entries at each reboot. When the PC is back and still stuck, the same finding continues (live); at 100 percent it resolves. A PC that cannot sync gets H5 after 24 hours of connected time, also when it is off every night. A PC that was off for a weekend and catches up in some minutes gets no H5.

**Tests.** `TestStuckDeviceIsHeldWhileAway` (open H5, disconnect, 30 hours past `stuck_sync`: same ID, stale, no resolved history entry; back below 100 percent: live; at 100 percent: resolves), `TestStuckDeviceCountsConnectedTime` (3 × 8 hours connected with nights off opens H5 only after more than 24 hours connected), `TestStuckDeviceOffForTheWeekendCatchesUp` (60 hours off at 99 percent, then synced in 5 minutes: no H5), `TestStuckDeviceHeldThroughRestarts` (server down and up, then a restart of syncwatch, while the PC is away: held, same record), `TestStuckDeviceHeldWhileAway` (rules), `TestLastCompletionRemembered` (model).

## 3. W-003: H7 and C2 wait while any server of the PC is unreachable

**Root cause.** During a VPN outage the near NAS loses the connections to the far site's PCs, and `Snapshot.Devices()` counts the far NAS's report "connected" only while that NAS is up. r0-c item 7 froze a cross-server finding only when every related server was stale, so H7 opened for PCs that synced with their own NAS the whole time.

**What changed.** `internal/engine/engine.go` (`freezer.frozen`, new `anyPresentStale`, `absenceCheck`): the absence-based findings H7 (PC offline) and C2 (inactive device) are frozen while any related server that is still in the configuration is stale (down, certificate changed, not contacted or not loaded since the start). An open H7/C2 stays as it is (stale) and no new H7/C2 opens. Servers that left the configuration do not count (r0-c item 6 closes or rewrites those findings). The presence-based cross-server findings (H8 for a device, X1, X2, C1) keep the r0-c item 7 rule (judged from the servers that are up, frozen only when all are stale); findings with a server (H5, H11, C3, H2) are unchanged.

**DECISION (re-opens r0-c item 7 for H7 and C2 only):** the absence of a PC is known only when every server that has it configured is reachable (contract C3). Trade-off, as in 78ef2d8: a PC that is really offline during a VPN outage gets H7 only after the outage; a PC that connects to a reachable NAS during the outage keeps its open H7 (stale) until the outage ends, then it resolves. A configured server that never answers (for example a wrong URL) blocks new H7/C2 for the PCs that it has configured until an administrator disables or removes it.
**Not covered:** a server that has not loaded since the start of syncwatch has no device list, so its PCs are not in `SeenBy` and do not freeze (the same as 78ef2d8).

**User-visible.** During a VPN outage the far site's PCs get no H7 "PC is offline"; open H7/C2 of those PCs show `stale` until the outage ends.

**Tests.** `TestPCOfflineWaitsForEveryServer` (the plan's case: the PC connected on B, disconnected on A, B down, 8 and 100 days later: no H7, no C2), `TestCrossFindingsFreezeOnlyWhenEveryServerIsStale` (adapted: H8 and X1 continue from A while B is down, H7 and C2 stay stale, no new H7 for a PC that passes `pc_offline` during the outage; everything stale with both down; H7 resolves and opens again only when B is back). `TestNewFindingsFromStaleServerWait` and `TestRemovedServerClosesItsFindingsQuietly` stay green.

## 4. W-005 (model part): a disabled server is a server, not a PC (contract C4)

**Root cause.** The collector manager leaves disabled servers out of the hub (r0-c's quiet close stays), but the other NAS still has their device configured. `Snapshot.Devices()` knew servers only from the hub, so a disabled SOL was a PC: H7 "PC SOL is offline" when it was switched off, C2/C3 later, and the PC counts included it.

**What changed.**
- `internal/model/model.go`: `Snapshot.Disabled []DisabledServer{ID, Name, MyID}`, `Snapshot.DisabledByDeviceID`. `Devices()` adds the stored device IDs of disabled servers after the polled servers with `IsServer`, the new `DeviceView.Disabled`, `ServerID` and the syncwatch name; like a PC, a disabled server is `Connected` when a polled server that is up has a connection to it, and gets `LastSeen` and the client version from the polled servers. `IsServerDevice()` and `DeviceName()` include disabled servers. `Snapshot.Clone` copies the list.
- `internal/engine/engine.go`: before each evaluation the engine sets `snap.Disabled` from `cfg.Servers[].Disabled` and `store.ServerDeviceID(id)` (`disabledServers`); the view's snapshot carries it, so the health line, the PC tile and `/api/v1/status` `devices[].server` see the server without code changes. The visible fingerprint includes the list.
- `internal/rules/health.go`: H6 for a folder that a device paused excludes only polled servers (`ServerByDeviceID`), because a disabled server does not report its own paused folders.

**DECISION:** H5, H6 (remote paused), H11 and C3 "does not share it back" report a disabled server as before (seen from the polled servers); H7, C2, C3 "never connected" and H8 for a device do not (contract C4).
**Limit:** a server that was never polled has no stored device ID and stays a PC until it is enabled and polled one time.

**User-visible.** Disabling a NAS no longer makes it a PC: no H7 "PC SOL is offline" when it is off, not counted as a PC; `/api/v1/status` `devices[]` shows it with `server: true`. On the Folders grid a disabled server is a server column; its cells show "–" (fixer 6 can leave it out, see the requests).

**Tests.** `TestDisabledServerIsNotAPC` (rules: no H7, C2, C3 never connected or H8; H6 and H11 stay; the device view; C3 for a PC that never connected as a control) and `TestDisabledServerIsNotAPC` (engine: the stored device ID comes from the store, the view's device list has no PC).

## 6. W-013: H5 never says 100 percent for a device that needs files

**Root cause.** `%.0f%%` (message) and `%.1f%%` (detail) printed 99.5 percent or more as 100: a PC that cannot store 18 files was "stuck at 100%".

**What changed.** `internal/rules/health.go` (`floorPercent`, `stuck`, `stuckDevice`): the percentage is cut to one decimal (`math.Floor(p*10)/10`), in the message without a trailing ".0" and in the `Completion` detail with one decimal; the message names the remaining items as the folder H5 does. Optional part done: a device at exactly 100 percent with remaining items (only directories, empty files or deletions missing) is stuck too.

**User-visible.** "Device PC-SHARED-ANNA is stuck at 99.9% of folder LIVE-FJORD-4FINALS for more than 24 hours (18 remaining items, seen from AKKA)"; detail `Completion` 99.9%.

**Tests.** `TestStuckDevicePercentIsNeverRoundedUp` (99.994 percent with 18 items: no "100" in message or detail; 40 percent; 100 percent with 3 items; 100 percent without items: no H5).

## 8. W-022: H2 waits 2 minutes for a link that could form only now

**Root cause.** H2 opened at once with `Since` = the time each server last saw the other. When a NAS was reachable again, or after a start, H2 opened as an ERROR in the evaluation in which H1 resolved and closed some seconds later, when Syncthing had connected the servers.

**What changed.** `internal/rules/health.go` (`links`, `linkGrace`): H2 has `MinDuration` 2 minutes, and `Since` is the latest of: the time each server last saw the other, Syncthing's start time on each server, and the time syncwatch reached each server again. `internal/model/model.go`, `hub.go`: new `Server.UpSince`, set by `Hub.Update` when the status becomes up (first contact after a start of syncwatch, or after a failure).

**DECISION:** the plan's grace (last seen and the start times) is extended with `UpSince`, because after a hang of a NAS or a VPN cut Syncthing does not restart and its start time is old: without it, H2 still opened when H1 resolved.
**Effect on real link drops:** a new drop opens H2 2 minutes after the drop (before: at once), so the urgent message comes 2 minutes later; a reconnect within 2 minutes gives no H2.

**Tests.** `TestLinkGraceAfterStart` (a link dropped an hour ago opens at once; no H2 in the evaluation in which H1 resolves; a start of Syncthing gives the grace, then H2 opens), `TestHubRecordsUpSince`; `TestFreezeWhileServerDown` now gives the servers a last-seen time (its H2 must open at once).
