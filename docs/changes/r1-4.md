## 1. W-018: "Send test message" does not test the role mention

Root cause: `SendTest` never set `Mention`; `Discord.Payload` adds `<@&role>` and `allowed_mentions.roles` only for
`Mention && RoleID != ""`.

What changed (`notifier.go`, `SendTest`): `Mention` is `cfg.Notify.DiscordRoleID != ""`. The summary says what the test shows:

- with a role: "Notifications from syncwatch reach this channel. This test message mentions the role, the same as urgent
  messages. If your phone did not notify you, check the role ID and that you have the role."
- without a role: "Notifications from syncwatch reach this channel. Urgent messages mention no role."

User-visible: with a role ID, the test message mentions the role (the members' phones notify). `message.go` is unchanged.

Test: `TestTestMessageMentionsTheRole` (payload on the wire: `<@&id>` first in `content` and `roles: [id]`; without a role no
mention and `"parse":[]`).

DECISION: every test message mentions the role when one is set. That is the point of the test, and the button is only for
administrators.

README (fixer 8): First start step 4 (README.md:120): "Select **Send test message** to check it. If you typed a role ID, the
test message mentions the role." The flash text in `settings.go:608` is fixer 5's.

## 2. W-024: the overnight summary lists an occurrence that was already announced

Root cause: `announced()` read the finding's current record. When the finding opens again after its "resolved" message, the
engine starts a new record (`PingedAt` and `ResolvedNotifiedAt` zero), so an older occurrence that got an urgent and a resolved
message looked unannounced. The history table has no `pinged_at` per occurrence, and the store is not this fixer's file.

What changed (`notifier.go`): the notifier keeps "announced runs" (finding ID, from, to: resolve times in Unix ms) in the
settings table under `notify.announced` (existing `JSONSetting`/`SetJSONSetting`, no schema change):

- a resolved message (also "Resolved during the night") records the run from its urgent message to the resolve it reports
  (`[PingedAt, ResolvedAt]` of the record, which covers the occurrences that carried `PingedAt` over);
- a line in "Opened and resolved during the night" or in the catch-up (item 3) records that one occurrence.

`announced(h)` checks these runs first, then the record as before. Runs older than 3 days are removed at the next write (the
overnight summary looks back at most about 36 hours). The list is read once per process and survives a restart.

User-visible: an occurrence that got its urgent and resolved messages is not listed again under "Opened and resolved during
the night" when the same finding opens again later (ops-chaos OC-10). The overnight snooze and ignore filter is unchanged
(moved into `hidden()`, shared with the catch-up).

Test: `TestOvernightSkipsEarlierAnnouncedOccurrence` (the persona's sequence in UTC: urgent and resolved messages, quiet hours
set with a start in the past, the finding opens again inside quiet hours past its debounce, a restart of the notifier, quiet
end: only "(19:15–19:25)" is listed).

DECISION: the per-occurrence state lives in the notifier (a settings key), not in a `history.pinged_at` column, because the
store is fixer 7's. If the integrator prefers a column later, `announcedRun` is the only reader to change.

## 3. W-007: urgent findings that open and resolve while notifications fail are never reported

Root cause: `urgent()` sends only open due records; "resolved" needs a ping (`PendingResolved`: `pinged_at<>0`); the
"opened and resolved" list existed only in the overnight summary.

What changed (`notifier.go`): `recordDelivery` keeps the time of the first failure of the current channels in `missed`
(memory only). A new webhook (`ChannelsChanged`) or a successful test message clears the banner but not `missed`. At the
next urgent send (`urgent()`), `missedBlips` reads the history since then: urgent occurrences that resolved at or after
`missed`, were due before they resolved (`OpenedAt + debounce(check) <= ResolvedAt`), are not announced (item 2) and are not
snoozed or ignored (now or when they resolved, as in the overnight summary). They go out in a section
"Opened and resolved while the notifications failed (HH:MM–HH:MM)" (the date is added to the start when the failure began on
another day):

- with urgent findings that are due now: one urgent message with the mention and the extra section;
- alone: a message without a mention, kind `resolved`, title "✅ Resolved while the notifications failed: {check title}:
  {subject}" or "✅ {count} urgent findings resolved while the notifications failed". Alone it is also the next try of a failing
  channel, and its success clears the banner.

After a successful send, the listed occurrences are recorded as announced (item 2) and `missed` is cleared. It is also cleared
when delivery works and nothing was missed. Without a channel nothing is recorded (`errNoChannel` returns before
`recordDelivery`), so `TestNothingMarkedSentWithoutChannel` is unchanged.

User-visible: after a Discord outage or a dead webhook, the channel gets the urgent findings that came and went during it,
once, without a mention (ops-chaos OC-03).

Tests: `TestCatchUpAfterFailure` (failing sender, H1 due and resolved, a shorter H1 on another server, sender back: one
message, no mention, one line "(10:00–10:20)", section "(10:05–10:45)", nothing more later), `TestCatchUpWithUrgentAfterNewWebhook`
(dead webhook replaced: one urgent message with the mention and the catch-up section), `TestCatchUpAndOvernightListEachOccurrenceOnce`
(a failure through quiet hours: the overnight summary lists the night's occurrence, the catch-up only the evening's one),
`TestHostileNamesAreNeutralised` now also renders the catch-up messages.

DECISION: the failure start is kept in memory only. A restart during an outage loses the catch-up (the plan's choice).
DECISION: instead of the plan's "skip entries whose due time is inside quiet hours", the overnight summary and the catch-up mark
what they list as announced, and the catch-up runs after the overnight summary in the same tick. Thus no occurrence is listed
twice, and an occurrence of an earlier night in a failure of more than one night (whose overnight summary was never sent) is
still reported.
DECISION: the failure start survives a new webhook and a successful test message, so the new webhook gets the catch-up.

README (fixer 8): the "A webhook that fails" bullet (README.md:307): "When the messages arrive again, syncwatch also sends the
urgent findings that opened and resolved while the notifications failed, without a mention. A restart of syncwatch during the
failure loses this list." plan.md:83 the same.

## 4. W-006: after a Discord outage syncwatch waits for its back-off (up to 30 minutes)

Root cause: `fail()` doubled the wait up to 30 minutes for every failure.

What changed (`discord.go`, `notifier.go`): `transient(err)` is true for an `httpError` with code 5xx or 429 and for every error
that is not an `unusableError` (timeouts, network errors, "too many requests after 3 tries", database errors). The new type
`unusableError` marks the errors that only a new webhook can end: the stored URL is not a Discord webhook, the URL is not valid,
the webhook cannot be decrypted (`errUndecryptable`; the texts are unchanged). `fail()` caps the wait at 5 minutes for transient
failures (1, 2, 4, 5, 5 … minutes, contract C7) and keeps 30 minutes for a refused webhook (HTTP 400, 401, 403, 404, and a 3xx
redirect) or an unusable one (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30 minutes). The reset on the first success and the immediate try after a new
webhook are unchanged.

User-visible: after a Discord outage the messages arrive at most 5 minutes after Discord works again (before: up to 30).

Tests: `TestFailingChannelBacksOff` now pins both classes (HTTP 404: 6 attempts in 65 minutes, HTTP 502: 14), `TestTransientFailures`.

DECISION: this changes a documented design (README.md:307 "1, 2, 4 … minutes and then every 30 minutes", plan.md:83). The
failure counter is shared by both classes.

README (fixer 8): README.md:307: "syncwatch tries again after 1, 2 and 4 minutes, then every 5 minutes while Discord or the
network is down, and up to every 30 minutes while Discord refuses the webhook (HTTP 400, 401, 403, 404)." plan.md:83 the same.

## 5. W-023: weekly report "New this week (N)" counts more than it lists

Root cause: `WeeklyMessage` counted every occurrence (INFO, snoozed and ignored ones included) but listed only ERROR and WARNING
lines, and listed ignored findings without a mark.

What changed (`notifier.go`, `WeeklyMessage`): both sections list and count only ERROR and WARNING occurrences of findings that
are not snoozed or ignored at the time of the report. A cut list still ends with "…and N more" (`limitLines`, and `Payload`
counts hidden lines correctly since r0-f).

User-visible: "New this week (5)" has 5 lines (or 8 lines and "…and N more"); an ignored or snoozed finding is not in the
weekly report, as in the overnight summary and in "Most important open findings".

Test: `TestWeeklyCountsWhatItLists` (10 WARNING + 1 ERROR new, 1 INFO, 1 ignored, 1 snoozed: "New this week (11)" with 8 lines
and "…and 3 more"; "Resolved this week (1)").

DECISION: snoozed and ignored findings are left out (not marked); INFO findings stay on the dashboard (the health line counts
them).

README (fixer 8): the Weekly report bullet (README.md:303): "new and resolved ERROR and WARNING findings of the week (without
snoozed and ignored findings)".

## 6. W-014 (title and lines): the grouped urgent title names no server or check

Root cause: `UrgentMessage` titled a group "🚨 N new urgent findings" and wrote one line per record, so the two S3 findings of
one nested directory on AKKA and SOL were two lines with nothing in the title.

What changed (`notifier.go`, `message.go`):

- Title of a group: "🚨 2 new urgent findings on AKKA, SOL: S3" (`where`: servers of the findings, `Server` and `Related`, in
  configuration order, removed ones last; check IDs in catalog order). One finding keeps "🚨 {check title}: {subject}".
- `urgentLines`: findings with the same checks, severity and stale flag whose subject and message are the same after their own
  server's name is taken out (word boundaries) share one line: the first one's line plus "_(also on SOL)_" before the details
  link. Findings about the server itself (subject = server name, for example H1) and cross-server findings keep their own lines.
  A line never names the same server twice.
- `message.go`: `detailsLink` (the existing link text, now shared by `findingLine` and `urgentLines`).

User-visible: a phone shows which servers and checks a grouped urgent message is about; one directory synced to both NAS gives
one line.

Tests: `TestUrgentMessageNamesServersAndMergesLines`, `TestUrgentMessageKeepsServerFindingsApart`.

DECISION: the merged line says "(also on SOL)" and not "… on AKKA and SOL": the share names differ per server
(`AKKA-LIVE-4FINALS on AKKA`, `SOL-LIVE-4FINALS on SOL`), so "on AKKA and SOL" would name a share that SOL does not have.
DECISION: the hold window that waits for findings that fall due seconds apart is not done (plan: deferred; it changes
README.md:299 "Findings that open in the same cycle go into one message"). Without it, the AKKA and SOL findings of one
directory still often come in two messages, because the structure scans of the two servers run at different times.

README (fixer 8): the Urgent message bullet (README.md:299): "The title of a message with more than one finding names the
servers and the checks. The same finding on more than one server has one line."

## 7. HEALTH (W-036, W-003 and W-005 parts): the health line

What changed (`notifier.go`, `HealthLine`, new `allKnown`):

- (a) Ignored findings count as "N ignored" (only when N > 0), after "N snoozed"; they no longer count as snoozed.
- (b) A server with status `cert-changed` shows as "SOL certificate changed", not "down".
- (c) Until a server answered a full check since the start (no server `Loaded`), the line says "PCs unknown" instead of
  "0 PCs, 0 offline more than …".
- (d) Contract C3: a PC that is not connected and was not seen within the PC offline threshold counts as offline only when
  every server in its `SeenBy` is up and loaded; otherwise it counts as unknown, shown as ", N unknown" when N > 0.
- (e) Contract C4: disabled servers (`cfg.Servers[].Disabled`) are in the total and named: "1 of 2 servers up (SOL disabled)".
  A disabled server that is still in the snapshot counts only as disabled.
- The order of the reasons: down, certificate changed, not contacted, disabled. The plural rules are unchanged.

Examples: `1 of 3 servers up (SOL certificate changed, OLD disabled) · 2 PCs, 1 offline more than 7 days, 1 unknown · ERROR 0 ·
WARNING 1 · INFO 0 · 1 snoozed · 1 ignored`; right after a start: `0 of 2 servers up (AKKA, SOL not contacted) · PCs unknown ·
ERROR 0 · WARNING 0 · INFO 0 · 0 snoozed`.

Tests: `TestHealthLineStates`; `TestScheduledMessagesWaitForFirstResults` now expects the disabled server in the count
(`1 of 3 servers up (SOL down, OLD disabled)`); `TestHealthLineWording` is unchanged.

DECISION: this re-opens the r0-g2 note (item 5) that `cert-changed` still counts as down.
DECISION: in this branch the device ID of a disabled server still counts as a PC (the snapshot does not know it). Fixer 3's
`Devices()` change (contract C4) makes it a server; this code needs no change for that.

Health-line text changes for the API changelog (`/api/v1/status` `health`, the Overview and the weekly report):
1. "· N ignored" after "N snoozed" when there are ignored findings; ignored findings are no longer in "N snoozed".
2. "(SOL certificate changed)" instead of "(SOL down)" for a server whose certificate changed.
3. Disabled servers are counted and named: "1 of 2 servers up (SOL disabled)" (before: "1 server up").
4. "PCs unknown" instead of "0 PCs, 0 offline more than …" until a server answered a full check after a start.
5. ", N unknown" after the offline count for PCs whose servers are not all up and loaded (they were counted as offline before).

README (fixer 8): the health line example in the Weekly report bullet (README.md:303) can stay; add: "The health line says
'certificate changed' and 'disabled' for those servers, 'PCs unknown' until a server answered after a start, and counts ignored
findings separately."

## 9. W-040 (log part): banner and log tell different stories

Root cause: a notice that Discord refused was always queued with the log line "cannot send the settings notice, syncwatch
retries it with the next notifications"; the retry at the next tick went to the same webhook and logged "settings notice
discarded, Discord refused it" (45 ms or 50 s later).

What changed (`notifier.go`): `Notice` and `NoticeLater` note the channel generation in which they built their senders. When
Discord refuses a notice (HTTP 4xx other than 429) and the channels were not replaced since (same generation after the send),
the notice is discarded at once with one line, "settings notice discarded, Discord refused it". In all other failures it is
queued with the "retries" line as before. A notice for a replaced webhook still goes to the new one (r0-f DECISION).

User-visible: one log line for each decision about a notice. Tests: `TestNoticeLogsOneDecision` (refused by the configured
webhook: one warning, one attempt; replaced webhook: one "retries" warning and the notice on the new webhook);
`TestRejectedNoticeIsDropped` now expects one warning and one attempt. The banner text is fixer 6's (contract C7).
